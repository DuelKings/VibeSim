1. What happens to the energy if I slowly pull the two atoms in H2 apart?
STATUS: COMPLETED (finite-basis electronic curve; not spectroscopic accuracy)
Starting near the bond minimum, pulling H2 apart raises its energy, progressively flattening toward two separate neutral H atoms. At compressed distances the repulsive energy rises as well. No activation barrier or rate is inferred.

Method: ORCA 6.1.1, spin-adapted full CI using ALL 10 (DZ) / 28 (TZ) orbitals active with 2 electrons; neutral singlet H2. One-electron neutral doublet H references use UHF in matching basis, exact for one electron within that basis. Full CI avoids restricted single-determinant dissociation artifacts. CI solution and normal termination checked; orbital optimization is unnecessary for a full orbital space. See outputs for full-CI warning (informational, expected).
Limitations: modest finite Gaussian bases, residual basis error and basis-set superposition effects; coarse distance grid (minimum not optimized), no complete-basis extrapolation, ZPE/D0, finite-temperature free energy, solvent, relativity or nuclear nonadiabatic effects. Report D_e, NOT D0 or activation energy. No fitted equilibrium claim or frequency minimum claim.
